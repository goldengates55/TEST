Get set up: MetaMask and OpenSea

New to digital wallets? Here is how to get what you need before you buy. Screens on these sites change from time to time, so follow their on-screen instructions and help pages.

1. Get a wallet (MetaMask)
  • Go to metamask.io and choose the browser extension (Chrome, Firefox, Brave or Edge) or the mobile app.
  • On a phone, install only from the official App Store or Google Play listing, and watch out for fake copies.
  • Open it, choose to create a new wallet and set a password.
  • Write down your Secret Recovery Phrase on paper and keep it offline. Anyone who has it can take everything in your wallet.
  • Add USDC or ETH to the wallet to cover the price and the network fee. MetaMask offers a prompt to fund your wallet after setup.

MetaMask's own setup guide

2. Open OpenSea
  • Go to opensea.io in your browser and bookmark it.
  • On a phone, OpenSea suggests using the website in your browser and adding it to your home screen. An app store version exists, but some features may not be available in it.
  • Select the wallet icon or Connect, choose MetaMask, approve the connection and sign the request. This logs you in, with no password needed.

OpenSea's guide to using it on mobile

3. Buy your parcel
Use the "Buy on OpenSea" button at the top of this page, check the collection name and price, and confirm the purchase in your wallet.
Stay safe
  • Never share your Secret Recovery Phrase or password. No one from MetaMask, OpenSea or this site will ever ask for it.

A digital asset made to be kept

The Digital Parcel of Land is designed as something to hold for the long term, not a quick purchase. Ownership is recorded on the Ethereum blockchain, so it doesn't depend on a single company's database. It can be transferred to another wallet at any time, which means you can pass it to a child, a relative or anyone you choose, and they can pass it on in turn.

Keep it for life
Hold the parcel as long as you like. It stays in your wallet until you decide to transfer or sell it.
Pass it on
Send it to a family member's wallet, as a gift now or as part of your plans for the future.
Protect your access
Only the person with the wallet's recovery phrase can move the token. Store it safely offline and make sure someone you trust knows how access would be passed on. If the phrase is lost, the parcel can't be recovered.

How a family member can receive it

While you are alive: gift it
Your family member sets up their own wallet and gives you their wallet address. You transfer the parcel to that address from your wallet or from OpenSea. A small network fee applies, and a transfer to the wrong address can't be undone, so check the address carefully, or send a very small test transaction first if your wallet allows it.
Later: leave it to them
The parcel can only be moved by someone who can access the wallet that holds it. Write down the wallet's details and recovery instructions, keep them somewhere safe such as a home safe or with your notary or lawyer, and tell your next of kin where to find them. Don't write the recovery phrase in a will itself, since wills can become public.
When they take over
The new owner should move the parcel into a wallet of their own right away, so the old wallet and its recovery phrase no longer matter.

Lasting for generations is our aim, not a guarantee. It depends on the Ethereum network, the services that display the artwork and the way you look after your wallet. To include the parcel in an estate plan or will, speak with a notary or lawyer.

A public record anyone can verify

Every valid transaction involving the Digital Parcel of Land is recorded on the Ethereum blockchain. Once confirmed, a record is not changed or deleted, and it stays on the public ledger for as long as the network runs. Anyone, at any time, from anywhere with an internet connection, can check who owns a parcel and see its transfer history. No account or permission is needed, and you don't have to rely on us to confirm it. Free tools called block explorers, as well as OpenSea itself, show this information.

A permanent record proves ownership and history. It may not show price, but indicates the transaction is valid and stored.
